About the Author

The Headmaster is the author of the Montavera books — the novels and the Black Index — which he writes in English and Spanish and files from his office in the city itself. The Montavera Chronicle records his decrees, his teachings, and his seal. He publishes under his own roof: Montavera Games, with the books issued by Montavera Press.

He has been writing since 2009. Below is the road, in photographs — and, beneath them, the receipts.

The Journey — In Photographs

I have been writing since 2009. Here is what the camera caught of the road — every photograph dated from the photograph itself.

2009

I wrote my first novel the year after my creative writing class at the University of St. Thomas. No photographs of it survive — it was never digitized; it lives in a manila folder in my garage. The camera starts catching up five years later.

November 5, 2014 — 6:41 AM

National Novel Writing Month, in the shirt, holding the handwritten journal. The habit was already a habit.

December 7, 2014

A language under construction in the back of that journal: an alphabet of my own devising — its letters, its joins, and the first notes on what a written name can do.

2015–2016

Teaching fourth grade, I started a writing club — and got fourteen of my students published. Then I called the Half Price Books stores around Houston and built them a book tour. Every student sold every copy of their book. They learned the craft that year, and then they learned the selling. I still have all of their books.

October 28, 2016

The noveling chart on my classroom wall — my own name at the top, set for 50,000 words, and my students’ chapter goals underneath. I have always taught the thing I do.

December 7, 2017

One desk, two jobs: my students’ work on one side, my novel’s chapter-and-scene binder on the other.

April 28, 2019

Camp NaNoWriMo, won — 50,899 words of THEOMACHY, an earlier novel of mine, finished and documented.

May 17, 2019

Red-pen edits on the Luna typescript, typed on my Hermes in a café. The scene closes in Barloma — a city of mine I later ran as a storytelling game.

January 28, 2022 — 4:41 AM

The writing hour, as it actually looks: the Freewrite, the coffee, the dark before school.

February 4, 2022

And here is this book being written — The Songs Beneath on the Traveler’s screen: Tomek, the empty fish trap, his father’s knife. 2,583 words on the counter that night.

November 5, 2026

This morning. First grade at Burnet Elementary. The books are real now; the desk is the same desk.

“My advice to the young writer is likely to be unpalatable in an age of instant successes and meteoric falls. I tell the neophyte: Write a million words — the absolute best you can write, then throw it all away and bravely turn your back on what you have written. At that point, you're ready to begin.”

— David Eddings. Ray Bradbury, Jerry Pournelle, and John D. MacDonald taught the same rule: a writer is made at a million words.

I didn't throw mine away — but I did write them. Below is the tally of a writing life: every major work, dated, counted, and opened just far enough to show what it is about — because what a writer loves to write is the real receipt. It is also the tale of an evolving writer: a kid selling a comic to his classmates becomes, line by line, the man who built Montavera.

How the tally is kept: every work is counted once, at its fullest form; where a book survives as a folder of chapter files, the folder is counted once, whole. Earlier drafts, alternate versions, and mirrored copies are excluded — counting them would multiply the number several times over, and they are not the claim. The two paper works at the top of the table were never digitized; they carry no count. They are part of the tale, not the tally — and they are where the tally learned to walk.
